Buying Your First Home in Ontario: A Step-by-Step Guide

By Rob Vettese · November 26, 2026 · 3 min read

Buying your first home is exciting, and it comes with a lot of new terms and numbers. Here's the process I walk my first-time buyers through, in the order it usually happens.

1. Know Your Down Payment Requirement

In Canada, the minimum down payment depends on the purchase price. According to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada and CMHC:

  • $500,000 or less: at least 5% of the price.
  • $500,000 to under $1.5 million: 5% of the first $500,000, plus 10% of the amount above that.
  • $1.5 million or more: at least 20%, and mortgage default insurance isn't available.

If you put down less than 20%, you'll need mortgage default insurance. The premium is usually added to your mortgage, but in Ontario the provincial sales tax on that premium is paid at closing, so budget for it.

2. Use the Savings Programs Available to You

Two federal programs can help you build your down payment faster:

  • First Home Savings Account (FHSA): you can contribute up to $8,000 a year, to a lifetime limit of $40,000. Contributions are tax-deductible, and qualifying withdrawals for a first home are tax-free. (CRA)
  • Home Buyers' Plan (HBP): lets eligible buyers withdraw up to $60,000 from their RRSP toward a home, which is then repaid to the RRSP over time.

Many buyers can use both for the same purchase. A financial advisor can help you decide how to combine them.

3. Get Pre-Approved Before You Shop

A mortgage pre-approval tells you what a lender is likely to offer and holds a rate for a period of time. It also shows sellers you're a serious buyer. Talk to your bank or a mortgage broker early, before you fall in love with a house that's outside your range.

4. Budget for Closing Costs

Your down payment isn't the only cash you'll need on closing day. Common costs include:

  • Land transfer tax: Ontario charges land transfer tax on every purchase. First-time buyers may qualify for a refund of up to $4,000 of the provincial tax (Ontario.ca). Toronto also charges its own municipal land transfer tax. The other areas I serve, including Niagara, Hamilton, Burlington and Markham, do not.
  • Legal fees and title insurance for your real estate lawyer.
  • Home inspection, if you include one in your offer.
  • Adjustments for property taxes or utilities the seller has prepaid.

5. Search, Tour and Make an Offer

Once you know your budget, we'll narrow down neighbourhoods and property types, then tour homes that fit. When you find the right one, we'll review recent comparable sales together so your offer is based on data, not guesswork. We'll also decide which conditions to include, such as financing or inspection.

6. From Firm Deal to Keys

After your offer is accepted and conditions are waived, your lawyer handles the title search and paperwork, your lender finalizes the mortgage, and you'll arrange home insurance. On closing day, your lawyer releases the funds and you get the keys.

Every purchase is different, and this is a general overview rather than financial or legal advice. If you're thinking about buying your first home in Niagara, Hamilton, Burlington or the GTA, I'm happy to walk you through it.
